Currently Not Collectible Status

When paying your IRS debt would leave you unable to meet basic living expenses, the IRS is required to halt all collection activity. This is called Currently Not Collectible status — and it can provide immediate, powerful relief.

IRS Hardship Status · IRM 5.16.1

What Is Currently Not Collectible Status?

Currently Not Collectible (CNC) is an IRS designation — governed by Internal Revenue Manual 5.16.1 — that halts all enforced collection action when a taxpayer's income is entirely consumed by allowable living expenses and they have no assets from which the IRS could collect.

When your account is placed in CNC status, the IRS suspends levies, garnishments, and seizures. You stop receiving collection notices. The tax debt does not go away — interest and penalties continue to accrue — but the IRS takes no active steps to collect during the CNC period.

CNC status is particularly powerful when combined with an awareness of the Collection Statute Expiration Date (CSED). Under IRC §6502, the IRS has 10 years from the date of assessment to collect a tax debt. If a taxpayer remains in CNC status and the CSED expires, the tax debt is legally extinguished and the IRS can no longer collect it. For clients with older tax debts and limited income, this strategy can result in the debt disappearing without any payment at all.

What CNC Status Means for You

  • All IRS levies and wage garnishments are released or suspended
  • No new levies can be issued while CNC is in effect
  • IRS collection calls and notices stop
  • The 10-year collection statute continues to run
  • Penalties and interest continue to accrue (but cannot be collected)
  • The IRS may review your status annually if your income increases

Eligibility

Who Qualifies for Currently Not Collectible Status?

The IRS evaluates your financial situation using the same Collection Financial Standards used for Offer in Compromise cases.

The Financial Analysis

To qualify for CNC status, your total monthly income must be equal to or less than your total allowable monthly expenses. The IRS calculates allowable expenses using:

  • National Standards — Fixed allowances for food, clothing, personal care, and miscellaneous expenses based on household size
  • Local Standards — Housing and transportation expenses based on Broward County, Florida norms
  • Other Necessary Expenses — Health insurance premiums, court-ordered payments, minimum credit card payments, childcare, and certain other expenses

If your allowable expenses meet or exceed your income, you likely qualify. The determination is made using Form 433-A (Collection Information Statement for Wage Earners and Self-Employed Individuals) or Form 433-F.

Situations That Often Lead to CNC

  • Recent job loss or significant reduction in income
  • Disability or serious medical condition
  • Fixed income (Social Security, pension) with high allowable expenses
  • Self-employment with irregular or declining revenue
  • Divorce or separation creating financial hardship
  • Retirees with limited liquid assets and modest income

CNC is not limited to taxpayers with zero income. Many working Floridians with modest incomes, high housing costs (particularly in Broward County), and significant medical expenses qualify. The analysis must be done correctly — an improperly prepared Form 433-A can cause a denial or an unnecessarily high payment demand.
