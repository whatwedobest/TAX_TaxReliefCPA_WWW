IRS Penalty Abatement

IRS penalties can add 25–47.5% or more to your underlying tax balance. In many cases those penalties can be legally removed — and the IRS is required to consider every valid abatement request.

Penalty Relief · IRC §6651, §6654, §6724

What Is IRS Penalty Abatement?

Penalty abatement is the process of requesting that the IRS remove or reduce penalties assessed against your tax account. The IRS assessed over $40 billion in civil penalties annually in recent years — and a significant portion of those penalties are removed each year for taxpayers who request abatement with proper justification.

The most common IRS penalties subject to abatement are:

  • Failure to File (IRC §6651(a)(1)) — 5% per month up to 25% of the unpaid tax
  • Failure to Pay (IRC §6651(a)(2)) — 0.5% per month up to 25% of the unpaid tax
  • Failure to Pay Estimated Tax (IRC §6654) — Calculated on underpayment of quarterly estimates
  • Accuracy-Related Penalty (IRC §6662) — 20% of the underpayment attributable to negligence or substantial understatement
  • Information Return Penalties (IRC §6721–6724) — Penalties for late or incorrect 1099s, W-2s, and other information returns

When both the failure-to-file and failure-to-pay penalties apply simultaneously, the combined maximum can reach 47.5% of the original tax owed — before interest is added. Penalty abatement alone can reduce your total IRS balance by tens of thousands of dollars.

Two Primary Abatement Grounds

1. First-Time Penalty Abatement (FTA)

The IRS will automatically abate the failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, and failure-to-deposit penalties for the first year in which they apply if the taxpayer: (a) has filed all required returns or filed an extension, (b) has paid or arranged to pay any tax due, and (c) has no prior penalties in the preceding three tax years. FTA is available by phone call and does not require written justification. It is the most powerful and underused penalty relief tool available.

2. Reasonable Cause Relief

For penalties not eligible for FTA — or for amounts beyond FTA — reasonable cause relief is available when the taxpayer exercised ordinary care and prudence but was unable to comply. Qualifying circumstances include: serious illness of the taxpayer or immediate family member, natural disaster, reliance on erroneous professional advice, or destruction of records. A written reasonable cause statement must be submitted with documented support.

Our Process

How We Request Penalty Abatement

01

Penalty Analysis

We pull your IRS account transcripts and identify every penalty assessed, when it was assessed, and which tax periods are involved. We then evaluate FTA eligibility and reasonable cause grounds for each penalty type.

02

FTA Request

Where eligible, we contact the IRS by phone to request First-Time Penalty Abatement — the fastest and most certain form of relief. FTA can often be approved in a single call and credited to your account within days.

03

Written Reasonable Cause

For penalties exceeding FTA eligibility or for years not covered by FTA, we prepare a written reasonable cause statement — citing IRS guidance, Revenue Rulings, and documented circumstances — and submit it with supporting evidence.

04

Appeals if Denied

If the IRS denies the initial abatement request, we file a protest with the IRS Office of Appeals. Appeals officers are independent of IRS Collections and frequently grant abatement that was denied at the service center level.
