IRS Levy & Wage Garnishment Release

An IRS levy is one of the most aggressive collection tools available. Time is critical — but with a licensed CPA representing you, levies can be released and collection stopped.

Emergency IRS Relief · IRC §6343

What Is an IRS Levy?

An IRS levy is the legal seizure of your property to satisfy a tax debt. Unlike a tax lien — which is a legal claim against property — a levy is the actual taking of that property. The IRS is authorized to levy wages, bank accounts, Social Security benefits, accounts receivable, and other assets.

A bank levy (Form 668-A) freezes funds on deposit on the day the levy is served. The bank holds those funds for 21 days and then turns them over to the IRS. That 21-day window is your opportunity to act.

A wage garnishment (Form 668-W) is a continuous levy that attaches to each paycheck until the debt is resolved. The IRS calculates the exempt amount (based on filing status and dependents using Publication 1494) and takes everything above that threshold — often 70–80% of take-home pay for single taxpayers.

Under IRC §6343, the IRS is required to release a levy when: the underlying liability is paid, the levy is creating an economic hardship, an installment agreement is entered, the collection period has expired, or the fair market value of the levied property exceeds the liability and releasing part of the levy would not hinder collection.

IRS Warning Signs — Act Immediately

  • You received IRS Notice CP504 (Final Notice before Levy)
  • You received IRS Letter 1058 or LT11 (Notice of Intent to Levy)
  • Your employer received IRS Form 668-W
  • Your bank froze funds due to Form 668-A
  • A Revenue Officer has contacted your employer or clients
  • You have unfiled returns and received a Substitute for Return (SFR)

The IRS must send a Final Notice of Intent to Levy (via certified mail to your last known address) and allow 30 days before levy. If you did not receive this notice, the levy may be procedurally defective and releasable on that basis alone.

Legal Grounds

How We Get Your Levy Released

There are multiple legal avenues to levy release. We pursue every applicable ground simultaneously to achieve the fastest result.

Primary Release Grounds (IRC §6343)

  • Installment Agreement — Entering a payment agreement is the most common release mechanism. The IRS is required to release a continuous wage levy once an agreement is in effect.
  • Currently Not Collectible Status — If paying would create economic hardship, the IRS must release the levy and place the account in CNC status.
  • Offer in Compromise Submission — Filing a valid OIC generally suspends levy action while the offer is under consideration.
  • Collection Due Process (CDP) Hearing — Filing Form 12153 within 30 days of the Final Notice triggers a CDP hearing, during which the IRS cannot levy while the case is pending before the IRS Office of Appeals or Tax Court.

Additional Release Strategies

  • Procedural Defects — If the IRS failed to send the Final Notice to your last known address, the levy may be released as improper.
  • Innocent Spouse Relief — If the tax debt belongs to your spouse or former spouse, you may qualify for relief under IRC §6015.
  • Hardship Levy Release — The IRS must release a levy that is "creating an economic hardship due to the financial condition of the taxpayer" under IRC §6343(a)(1)(D). This requires documented financial hardship.
  • Expired Collection Statute — If the 10-year collection statute under IRC §6502 has expired, the levy is invalid and must be released.

See also: Currently Not Collectible Status, Installment Agreements, Offer in Compromise.

The IRS can seize a principal residence to satisfy a tax debt, but doing so requires approval from a federal district court judge (IRC §6334(a)(13)). This is rare and typically reserved for cases involving large balances, significant equity, and a taxpayer who has refused all other resolution options. In practice, the IRS pursues financial accounts and wages far more frequently than real property seizure.

Under IRC §6334, certain property is exempt from levy: unemployment benefits, workers' compensation, certain annuity and pension payments, a portion of wages (calculated per Publication 1494), minimum exemption for personal effects and tools of trade, undelivered mail, and certain assistance under the Job Training Partnership Act. We identify all applicable exemptions before any IRS levy is resolved.
