IRS Installment Agreements

When you can't pay your IRS debt in full, a negotiated installment agreement stops collection action and gives you a structured, affordable path to resolution.

IRS Payment Plans · IRC §6159

What Is an IRS Installment Agreement?

An Installment Agreement (IA) is a formal arrangement between a taxpayer and the IRS — authorized under Internal Revenue Code §6159 — that allows an outstanding tax liability to be paid over time in regular monthly installments.

Once an installment agreement is in effect, the IRS generally suspends enforced collection actions such as bank levies and wage garnishments. A federal tax lien that has been filed is not released until the balance is paid in full, but new lien filings are typically deferred for taxpayers who enter agreements under the streamlined thresholds.

It is critical to understand that penalties and interest continue to accrue on the unpaid balance throughout the agreement. Proper structuring — including exploring penalty abatement before entering the IA — can significantly reduce the total cost of the agreement.

Types of Installment Agreements

  • Guaranteed IA — Available if you owe $10,000 or less (excluding penalties/interest), have filed all returns for the past 5 years, and can pay within 3 years. The IRS must accept under IRC §6159(c).
  • Streamlined IA — For balances up to $50,000 (individuals) or $25,000 (businesses), payable within 72 months. No financial disclosure required.
  • Non-Streamlined / Full Financial Analysis IA — For balances over $50,000 or when longer terms are needed. Requires Form 433-A or 433-F and full income/expense disclosure.
  • Partial Payment IA (PPIA) — Monthly payment is less than the full balance; remaining balance may expire at end of the 10-year collection statute under IRC §6502.

Eligibility

Which Agreement Type Is Right for You?

The right structure depends on your total balance, financial situation, and whether you want to minimize total payout over time.

Requirements for All Installment Agreements

  • All required federal tax returns must be filed before the IRS will enter an agreement
  • Current year estimated tax payments (if self-employed) must be current
  • Any current year withholding must be adequate
  • The taxpayer must remain current with all future tax obligations during the agreement

Default on any of these conditions causes the IRS to terminate the agreement and resume full collection activity. Ronald A. Muscarella helps clients establish ongoing compliance systems to protect their agreement.

The PPIA Advantage for Large Balances

A Partial Payment Installment Agreement (PPIA) is a powerful but underused option. Unlike a standard IA, the monthly payment under a PPIA is set at whatever you can afford based on your actual financial disclosures — even if that amount will never fully pay off the debt. Any remaining balance at the end of the 10-year collection statute (IRC §6502) legally expires and cannot be collected.

The IRS does require a financial review of PPIA arrangements every two years. We prepare those reviews proactively to maintain the agreement at the most favorable payment amount.

For clients who owe $100,000–$500,000 and cannot qualify for an Offer in Compromise, a PPIA combined with strategic penalty abatement can dramatically reduce total lifetime payments. See also: Penalty Abatement and Offer in Compromise.

Our Process

How We Negotiate Your Installment Agreement

01

Transcript Review

We obtain your IRS account transcripts (Forms 4340, IMFOLT, AMDISA) to verify the exact balances assessed, confirm all returns are filed, and identify any pending collection actions.

02

Financial Analysis

For non-streamlined cases, we prepare Form 433-A (Collection Information Statement) using IRS National and Local Standards to maximize allowable expenses and minimize your required monthly payment.

Penalty Abatement First

Before entering any agreement, we assess whether first-time penalty abatement or reasonable cause relief under IRC §6651 and §6654 can reduce the underlying balance — potentially saving thousands.

04

Agreement & Protection

We negotiate the agreement terms with IRS Collections, file Form 9465 (Installment Agreement Request) or negotiate directly with a Revenue Officer, and confirm in writing that enforced collection is suspended.
