Payroll Tax Resolution

Delinquent payroll taxes are among the most aggressive IRS collection priorities — and the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty can make business owners personally liable. Expert CPA representation is essential.

Business Tax Crisis · IRC §6656, §6672

Understanding IRS Payroll Tax Problems

Payroll taxes — comprising employee income tax withholding, employee Social Security and Medicare (FICA), and the employer's matching FICA contribution — are collected and remitted to the IRS by employers through the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System (EFTPS) on a regular deposit schedule.

When a business fails to make these deposits, the IRS assesses a Failure to Deposit penalty under IRC §6656 — ranging from 2% to 15% of the undeposited amount depending on how late the deposit is made. For significant delinquencies, the IRS assigns a Revenue Officer who has broad powers: the authority to show up at your business, demand books and records, interview employees, and initiate seizure of business assets.

The most serious risk, however, is the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty (TFRP) under IRC §6672.

The Trust Fund Recovery Penalty — Personal Liability

The "trust fund" portion of payroll taxes — employee income tax withholding and the employee share of FICA — is considered money held in trust for the government. If a business fails to remit these funds, the IRS can assert the TFRP against any "responsible person" who "willfully" failed to ensure the taxes were paid. This means:

  • The penalty is 100% of the unpaid trust fund taxes
  • It is assessed personally — against the individual, not the business entity
  • It survives business bankruptcy and dissolution
  • Multiple responsible persons can be assessed for the same liability
  • It attaches to personal assets — home, bank accounts, wages

A responsible person is anyone with signing authority, check-signing authority, or the power to direct disbursement of business funds — including owners, officers, and in some cases, bookkeepers and accountants.

Trust Fund Defense

Defending the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty

Two Elements the IRS Must Prove

The IRS must establish both (1) that you were a responsible person and (2) that your failure to remit was willful. These are separate elements, and challenging either can defeat or reduce the TFRP assessment.

Responsible Person Defense

Not everyone with a title is a responsible person. The IRS evaluates who actually had the authority and duty to ensure taxes were paid. A person who had signing authority but was not involved in financial decisions, or who was controlled by another person with ultimate authority, may not be a responsible person under the law. We document and present the actual decision-making structure during the TFRP interview.

Willfulness Defense

Willfulness under IRC §6672 requires either intentional disregard of a known legal duty or reckless disregard for whether a duty existed. A person who was unaware of the delinquency, was actively deceived by a bookkeeper or co-owner, or who took reasonable steps to ensure taxes were paid may not have acted willfully. We document the facts of each case to challenge the willfulness finding.

The TFRP Interview Process

Before assessing the TFRP, the IRS Revenue Officer conducts an interview using IRS Form 4180 — a detailed questionnaire about your role in the business, your authority over finances, and your knowledge of the tax delinquency. This interview is recorded and used as the basis for the personal assessment.

You should never attend a Form 4180 TFRP interview without representation. Anything you say becomes part of the IRS record and can be used to support the assessment. Ronald A. Muscarella attends all TFRP interviews, prepares clients thoroughly, and ensures that the factual record supports the strongest possible defense.

If the TFRP is assessed, you have the right to appeal within 60 days via written protest — and further appeal to the IRS Office of Appeals. The TFRP is also subject to a separate Collection Due Process (CDP) appeal right under IRC §6330 when levy action is proposed.
